## The audit, and what it is for

Opened per the plan item's own instruction: **audit before rendering**. The
check is one grep and it has reversed a document's premise three times in this
project — `[OMTI]` §5 was recorded as "owed" while the code cited 37
subsections; `[6840]`'s item said "no record" while the model derived sixteen
sections; `[8259]`'s record claimed the "whole programming model" at 11 of 24
pages. The point is to tell **`derived`** (taken from the document, field by
field, with the text quoted) from **`walked`** (a page-by-page pass) from
**genuinely unread** — because only the third needs rendering, and the second
is where this project's defects have actually been.

## The three gaps that are not merely unread

**§2, Data Organization and Addressing Capabilities — and the addressing modes
came from somewhere else.** `ap_m68030_ea.h` derives the whole effective-address
decode from **`[PRM]` §2** — Figure 2-2 and Tables 2-1 through 2-4, named
explicitly — and cites nothing from `[030]`'s own §2, which covers the same
subject for this exact processor. That is not wrong: the plan records that the
PRM's figures "are all intact, so nothing here is derived", which was a
deliberate choice about scan quality. But it means **the processor's own chapter
on its own addressing modes has never been opened**, and `CLAUDE.md`'s
resolution order puts the part's own manual first precisely because a sibling
"often carries the note that resolves an ambiguity in ours".

**§3, Instruction Set Summary — same shape.** The instruction work cites
`[PRM]` Table 8-2 and the per-instruction pages throughout. `[030]` §3 is the
68030's own summary of the same set, including whatever it says about the
instructions the 68030 adds or changes. Never consulted.

**§5, Signal Description — and this one is structural.** `ap_m68030_bus.c` is
built from §7, *Bus Operation*, with 21 citations. §5 is the chapter that
**defines the signals §7 operates on**. A bus model derived from the timing
chapter without the signal chapter is exactly the arrangement that let the write
strobes be wrong until `[030]` §7.3.2 was transcribed — recorded in
`COMPLETION_PLAN.md` as "the tail was recorded as one difference (DS) and turned
out to be three". §5 is where `DSACK`, `STERM`, `CIIN`, `CBREQ`, `AVEC` and the
rest are specified.

*Sections 12, 13 and 14 and Appendix A are expected to be applications,
electrical and mechanical* — but that is a statement about the **contents page**,
not about the pages. `[8259]` hid `TJLJH` in a range dismissed as electrical and
`[8237]` hid two rule pages in one dismissed as mechanical, both in this same
session. They get read.

## Verdict, and the shape of the job

**§7, §8, §9 and §11 are `derived`** — 88 of the 90 citations — and a
field-by-field pass over them is a *verification*, not a discovery. That is
worth doing (it is what found `ST3` in `[OMTI]` §6.4 and Register B in
`[146818]`), but it is second in priority.

**§2, §3 and §5 are genuinely unread, and §5 is load-bearing for a module this
project has already had a defect in.** They come first.

*Estimated pages*: §2 ~30, §3 ~30, §5 ~40 — about 100 pages for the unread
core, against 608 for the whole. The audit has therefore turned "render 608
pages" into "render 100, then verify 400" — which is the whole reason it is the
first step.

## §5, SIGNAL DESCRIPTION — WALKED, 12/12, 2026-08-25

PDF 127-138, document pages 5-1 to 5-12. Read as images. The first section of
this manual anyone has walked, and it was chosen because `ap_m68030_bus.c` is
built from §7's *timing* without the chapter that defines the signals §7 uses.

**Table 5-1's signal index and Table 5-2's signal summary are the whole of it** —
every signal with its mnemonic, direction, active state and whether it
three-states. Captured in full: `FC0`-`FC2`, `A0`-`A31`, `D0`-`D31`,
`SIZ0`/`SIZ1`, `OCS`, `ECS`, `R/W`, `RMC`, `AS`, `DS`, `DBEN`,
`DSACK0`/`DSACK1`, `STERM`, `CIIN`, `CIOUT`, `CBREQ`, `CBACK`, `IPL0`-`IPL2`,
`IPEND`, `AVEC`, `BR`, `BG`, `BGACK`, `RESET`, `HALT`, `BERR`, `CDIS`,
`MMUDIS`, `REFILL`, `STATUS`, `CLK`.

### Four rules this core already obeys, three of them uncited

- **§5.7.1: "`CIIN` is ignored during all write cycles."** `ap_m68030_access.c`
  consults `board_inhibits` at three places, **all in the read path**; the write
  path never reads it. Correct — and correct *by construction* rather than by
  citation, which is the kind of thing that survives until someone changes the
  write path for another reason.
- **§5.11.1: "`CDIS` does not flush the data and instruction caches; entries
  remain unaltered and become available again when `CDIS` is negated."**
  `cache_disable` gates `ap_m68030_cache_enabled` and flushes nothing. Correct,
  uncited.
- **§5.6.6**: "During two-clock synchronous write cycles, the MC68030 **does
  not assert `DS`**." The bus model has `AP_M68030_TERM_STERM` and the
  synchronous path; whether it suppresses `DS` on that path specifically is a
  §7 question and is listed below rather than guessed at here.
- **§5.10.1**, the `RESET` instruction: "resets external devices only; **the
  internal state of the processor is not altered**." Modelled, and the manual's
  own wording is now cited beside it.

### One stale comment, corrected

`ap_m68030_step.c`'s `RESET` arm said counting the reset "is the whole of the
observable effect **until there are devices to reset**". There are:
`ap_machine.c` watches `external_resets` and calls `ap_board_reset_devices` when
it moves. The instruction has reached the board's reset line since that was
wired, and the CPU-side comment was never updated — the code-comment form of the
staleness `check_docs.py` catches in the living documents and cannot catch here.

### One signal not modelled at all

**`MMUDIS`** — Table 5-1 lists it as an input and §5.11.2 defines it: it
"dynamically disables the translation mechanism of the MMU", and "the assertion
of `MMUDIS` does **not** flush the address translation cache; ATC entries become
available again when `MMUDIS` is negated". Zero occurrences in `src/`.

*Almost certainly unreachable, and the same shape as the SCN2681's `0x0C`*: it
is an emulator-support pin, and nothing in `src/core/board` or
`src/core/machine` drives either it or `CDIS`, so both are tied inactive on this
machine. Named in the plan rather than modelled, with the note that if `CDIS`
ever becomes drivable then `MMUDIS` should arrive with it — they are the same
paragraph of the manual and the same kind of pin.

## §2, DATA ORGANIZATION AND ADDRESSING CAPABILITIES — WALKED, 40/40, 2026-08-25

PDF 47-86, document pages 2-1 to 2-40. Read as images.

**The premise of walking it was that `ap_m68030_ea.h` derives the entire
effective-address decode from `[PRM]` §2 and had never opened the processor's
own chapter on the same subject. The result is that the two agree** — and that
is worth more than a defect would have been, because the decision to use
`[PRM]`'s figures was made on scan quality and has now been checked rather than
trusted.

### §2.5 is the 68030's own version of the tables we took from `[PRM]`

- **Figure 2-4**, *Effective Address Specification Formats* — the brief and full
  extension words with `D/A`, `REGISTER`, `W/L`, `SCALE`, `BS`, `IS`, `BD SIZE`
  and `I/IS`. **`BD SIZE` `00` = Reserved**, `01` Null, `10` Word, `11` Long,
  which is exactly what `AP_M68030_BD_RESERVED = 0` records and what the plan
  says was "Reserved, not null".
- **Table 2-1**, *IS-I/IS Memory Indirection Encodings* — all sixteen rows.
  `IS`=0 with `I/IS`=`001` is *Indirect Preindexed*; `IS`=1 with the same `001`
  is *Memory Indirect*. That is precisely the trap the plan records from
  `[PRM]` Table 2-2 — "a decoder that reads I/IS alone silently produces the
  wrong addressing mode" — stated here as its own table. Both reserved regions
  (`IS`=0/`I/IS`=`100`, and `IS`=1/`I/IS`=`100`-`111`) are present, and
  `AP_M68030_INDIRECT_RESERVED` covers them.
- **Table 2-2**, *Effective Addressing Mode Categories* — data, memory, control,
  alterable, per mode. All four are `ap_m68030_category.h`'s.

### Three rules checked against the code, all holding

- **§2.4.4/§2.4.5, and §2.8.1 a third time**: "If the address register is the
  stack pointer and the operand size is byte, the address is incremented
  [decremented] by **two rather than one**." `ap_m68030_address_step` tests
  `reg == 7u && operand_size == 1u`, with the sentence quoted and the note that
  A7 is the stack pointer whichever of the three it names — which is what
  §2.8.1's "the *active* stack pointer is automatically decremented or
  incremented by two" confirms.
- **§2.3's bit-field operands**: offsets "may have values in the range of
  **−2³¹ to 2³¹−1**", widths 1 to 32, and the memory field does *not* wrap where
  a register one does. `bitfield_spec_t.offset` is `int32_t` and the two wrap
  behaviours are modelled separately, with the register case cited to `[PRM]`
  §1.7.1 and commented "modelling both the same way is wrong in one of them".
- **§2.2.3's control-register access rules**: "for all CCR operations, the upper
  byte is read as all zeros and is ignored when written" — `ap_m68030_write_ccr`
  and `read_ccr` both mask to `AP_M68030_CCR_MASK`; and SFC/DFC have "only bits
  2:0 implemented ... the upper 29 bits are read as zeros and are ignored when
  written" — the write path masks `& 0x7u`.

### One bound stated here and not modelled

§2.5: "**The longest instruction for the MC68030 contains 10 extension words.**
It is a MOVE instruction with full format extension words for both the source
and destination effective addresses and with 32-bit base displacements and
32-bit outer displacements for both addresses." Nothing in `src/` enforces a
maximum. Not a defect — the decoder consumes what each mode specifies and a
longer instruction cannot be encoded — but it is a stated invariant that no
assertion checks, and it is the kind of bound a fuzzer would want.

*Also captured*: §2.1's operand types (bit, bit field 1-32, byte, word, long,
quad); §2.2's register organisation including quad words in any two data
registers "without restrictions on order or pairing"; §2.3's big-endian memory
layout and "instruction words must be aligned on word boundaries" against data
that need not be; §2.6's programmer's view; §2.7's family compatibility note
that `SCALE` = 0 is the one encoding common to both extension-word formats; and
§2.8's stacks and queues.

## §3, INSTRUCTION SET SUMMARY — WALKED, 32/32, 2026-08-25

PDF 87-118, document pages 3-1 to 3-32. The third and last of the sections the
audit found genuinely unread.

**Same result as §2: it confirms.** The instruction work cites `[PRM]`
throughout and never `[030]` §3; the two agree.

- **Table 3-13, Conditional Tests** — the sixteen conditions with their
  encodings and boolean formulae, `T` = `0000` and `F` = `0001` footnoted "*Not
  available for the Bcc instruction*". That is exactly what the branch item
  records from `[PRM]` Table 3-19: conditions 0 and 1 decode as `BRA` and `BSR`
  because `Bcc` cannot take them.
- **Table 3-12, Condition Code Computations**, two sheets — every instruction
  against `X`,`N`,`Z`,`V`,`C` with the special definitions spelled out as
  boolean expressions, including the shift/rotate `r`=0 rows. This is the
  definitive cross-check for `ap_m68030_alu.c` and is now on the shelf as such.
- **Tables 3-1 to 3-11 and 3-14** — data movement, integer arithmetic, logical,
  shift/rotate, bit manipulation, bit field, BCD, program control, system
  control, MMU and multiprocessor operations, then the whole alphabetised set
  across five sheets.

### Three rules checked against the code, all holding

- **§3.2.4**: a register shift count is taken "**modulo 64**".
  `ap_m68030_step.c` computes `d[count] % 64u` with the sentence quoted.
- **Table 3-6's note**: "All bit field instructions set the N and Z bits as
  shown for BFTST **before** performing the specified operation." The bit-field
  dispatch computes the flags from the field "as *found*, before any
  modification" and then switches on the operation — the ordering the note
  requires.
- **§3.5.3** restates §2.3's bit-field offset range, −2³¹ through 2³¹−1, a third
  time. Modelled as `int32_t`.

### One documented effect that is vacuous here, now said so

**§3.5.4**: `NOP` "forces synchronization of the integer unit pipeline by
waiting for all pending bus cycles to complete." This core executes strictly in
order and accounts each bus cycle to the instruction that issued it, so nothing
can be in flight when `NOP` runs and there is no state for a synchronisation to
wait on — correct by construction, the same shape as `CIIN` being ignored on
writes. `NOP` was a bare `return true` with no comment; it now carries the
citation and the condition under which it stops being vacuous (a store buffer,
or any out-of-order completion).

*Also noted, so it is not read as a contradiction*: §3.1 says an instruction may
be "as many as **11 words**" where §2.5 says "**10 extension words**". They
agree — one counts the operation word and the other does not.

## §1, §4 and §6 — WALKED, 2026-08-25

PDF 29-46 (§1, 18 pages), 119-126 (§4, 8) and 139-162 (§6, 24). All three were
`sampled` — one or two citations each — and are now read through.

**§1 corrects a claim in the plan.** Figure 1-4, the Status Register, **renders
perfectly legibly at 150 dpi**. A Phase 2 item says `ap_m68030_regs.c` used
`[PRM]` §1.3.2's Figure 1-8 "whose Figure 1-8 survives the scan where the 68030
manual's does not" — that is wrong, and was evidently decided without rendering
the page. The layout matches `AP_M68030_SR_*` bit for bit (T1 15, T0 14, S 13,
M 12, I 10-8, X 4, N 3, Z 2, V 1, C 0), and `AP_M68030_SR_DEFINED = 0xF71F` is
exactly §2.2.3's "only 12 bits of the status register are defined". Following
`[PRM]` was harmless; the reason given for it was not true.

*Also from §1, both already modelled with their sentences quoted*: §1.7's
"**22-Entry Fully Associative ATC**" (`AP_M68030_ATC_ENTRIES 22`), and §1.9's
caches of "256 bytes ... organized as 16 entries, each containing a block of
four long words" (`AP_M68030_CACHE_LINES 16`). Table 1-1's eighteen addressing
modes and Table 1-2's instruction list are §2 and §3's tables in summary form.

**§4** gives Table 4-1's address-space encodings (the one citation this section
had), the privilege levels and how they change, §4.3's exception vectors and
Figure 4-1's general stack frame. Notable and already right: "when the exception
being processed is an interrupt and the M bit is set, the M bit is cleared,
putting the processor into the interrupt mode"; and `STOP` leaves the processor
"stopped, not halted" — a normal processing state without bus cycles.

**§6** is the fullest of the three and confirms throughout. §6.1's five
conditions for an access to be cachable; `CDIS` disabling regardless of `CACR`;
`CIIN` per-bus-cycle and `CIOUT` per-page; §6.1.1/§6.1.2's tag and valid-bit
structure; **§6.1.2.1's write allocation**, whose two `WA` cases
`ap_m68030_cache.h` quotes verbatim; §6.1.2.2's rule that "the read portion of a
read-modify-write cycle is always forced to miss in the data cache", which
`ap_m68030_cache.c` gates on; and §6.1.3's single-entry and burst fill modes with
the six conditions under which `CBREQ` is not asserted.

*One rule worth having that this record had not captured*: "Read accesses from
the CPU address space and address translation table search accesses are **not
stored in the data cache**."

## §7, BUS OPERATION — WALKED, 106/106, 2026-08-26

PDF 163-268. The largest section, holding 21 of the 90 citations, and the first
of the four `derived` sections to be verified rather than discovered. **It
confirms throughout, and three of the confirmations are of fixes this project
made the hard way.**

### The write-cycle strobes, verified state by state

`COMPLETION_PLAN.md` records the write-timing item as "the tail was recorded as
one difference (DS) and turned out to be three". §7.3.2 gives the states, and
all three are there:

- **S1**: "the processor asserts `AS` ... The processor also asserts **`DBEN`**
  during S1" — where the read cycle asserts `DBEN` in S2.
- **S3**: "The processor asserts **`DS`** during S3, indicating that the data is
  stable on the data bus" — where a read asserts it in S1.
- **S5**: "negates `AS` and `DS` ... R/W, SIZ0-SIZ1, FC0-FC2, and **`DBEN` also
  remain valid throughout S5**" — where a read negates `DBEN` in S5.

§7.1.5 and §7.1.6 state the same two rules as prose, so the section says it
twice. The read cycle's states confirm the other half: S1 `AS`+`DS`, S2 `DBEN`,
**S4 "samples `CIIN` at the beginning ... at the end of S4 the processor latches
the incoming data"** — which is `ap_m68030_bus.h`'s comment verbatim — and S5
negating all three.

### The pipe's deferred bus error, verified

§7.5.1: "If a bus error occurs on an instruction fetch, the processor **does not
take the exception until it attempts to use that instruction word**. Should an
intervening instruction cause a branch or should a task switch occur, the bus
error exception does not occur." That is exactly what `pipe_suite` pins — "the
status bit travelling with its word, so a bus error faults where the word is
*used*, not where it was fetched".

### Cycle lengths, confirmed

§7.2.9's three-clock minimum for `DSACKx` termination and §7.2.10's two-clock
minimum for `STERM` are `AP_M68030_TERM_DSACK` and `AP_M68030_TERM_STERM`. The
`NOTE` that "`STERM` and `DSACKx` should **never** be asserted during the same
bus cycle" is satisfied by construction here, the termination being one enum.

### Four rules worth having that the earlier sections did not give

- **§7.2.9 extends §5.7.1's `CIIN` rule**: it is ignored "when the appropriate
  cache is not enabled or when cache inhibit out (`CIOUT`) is asserted. It is
  also ignored during write cycles **or translation table searches**." The
  table-search case is new.
- **§7.6 gives `NOP` a concrete purpose** that §3.5.4 only names — an external
  write whose `BERR` controls program flow — and then says it "is an irregular
  situation ... not required by most systems", and unnecessary even with
  error-detection circuitry "since the MMU always checks the validity of write
  cycles before they proceed to the data cache". Added to the `NOP` comment.
- **§7.4.1.2**: an autovector's number is "the sum of the interrupt level plus
  24 ($18)", and §7.4.1.3 gives the spurious-interrupt vector as **24**.
- **§7.5.4**: a double bus fault leaves the processor asserting `STATUS`
  continuously, and "**only an external reset operation can restart a halted
  processor**" — though bus arbitration still occurs.

*Also captured*: Tables 7-1 to 7-9 in full — `DSACK` codes, `SIZ` encodings,
byte-enable generation, memory-alignment bus-cycle counts, and the six
`DSACK`/`BERR`/`HALT` and `STERM`/`BERR`/`HALT` termination cases; §7.3.3's
twelve-state read-modify-write; §7.3.7's burst states with the three abort
conditions; §7.4's CPU-space encodings; and §7.7's bus-arbitration state
machine.
